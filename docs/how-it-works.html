JobFinder · how it works

How 3,120 job hits become one ranked list

A Python app on my laptop collects offers from four job boards and a night-time web agent, filters them, scores them against my CV and learns from how I rate them. The numbers come from the search on 9 November 2026.

What each part does

STEP 1

Collect

One search_<site>() function per board downloads result pages and parses them with BeautifulSoup. All of them return the same dict, with an id like profesia:5368676. A shared fetcher waits 0.8 s between requests and retries on 429 and 5xx errors.

STEP 2

Filter and merge

The title must contain an IT word and none of senior, manager, sales, SAP and similar. Offers older than 14 days drop out (30 for student roles). The same offer on two boards is merged on title, first word of the company and city.

STEP 3

Read the offer

Each offer page is downloaded once and cached. Regexes find the Requirements and Role sections in Slovak, Czech and English, so the report shows a short summary without opening the page.

STEP 4

Score

Fixed rules come first: skill matches from my CV (capped at 35), +45 for intern or part-time roles, penalties for full-time, German or years of experience. Then three capped adjustments: my stars, my notes and Claude's CV match.

STEP 5

Report and server

Python writes one HTML page with the offers as JSON and plain JavaScript. A standard-library server shows it on 127.0.0.1:8765 with a small JSON API. It checks Host and Origin headers and is not reachable from the network.

NIGHT

Web agent

On scheduled nights, headless Claude follows web_search_brief.md: it searches company career pages, opens each offer to check it is open, and saves it as JSON. Offers are merged by URL and go through the same filter and score.

LOOP

Learning from the tracker

Stars and statuses become weights for title words, skills and companies. Claude turns my notes into regex rules such as “Thesis position −25”. Words I keep rating highly are added as search terms.

MAIL · NOT LIVE YET

Mail sorter

Reads the job mailbox over IMAP without marking anything read. Claude sorts each message (interview, rejection, needs a reply…) and the tracker status moves on its own. Tested on sample mails, not yet on the real inbox.

The fixed rules

Points from config.toml. Who the role is for weighs more than how well the stack fits, so a part-time internship beats a full-time job with a perfect stack.
